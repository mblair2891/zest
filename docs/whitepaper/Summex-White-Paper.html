1. Executive summary

Summex is a hospitality operating system for a single restaurant or bar, a host venue where more than one operator can appear on a single guest check, or a multi-unit group. It is powered by Quantum Reach. Guest-facing cards run exclusively through Quantum Payments.

The product combines service (floor, sections, kitchen and bar routing), stations (order / ODS / host), money movement (each entity is its own Quantum Payments merchant; one guest tender; split capture; receipts by vendor; period settlement on a first-party ledger), and house money (cash drawers and banks, mix-based tip-out, first-party gift ledger).

The guest pays once, under the host name. Operators are paid from merchandise share on a period ledger — not from a second terminal at the table. Software billing is separate from guest card processing.

This paper describes how the system works today. Items that are not built are marked Roadmap. No processor rates are guaranteed. No bank partners are named. No compliance seals are claimed. The in-product Operators Guide is the operator manual.

2. The problem

Hospitality software still treats a dining room like a retail checkout, and a food hall like several unrelated shops.

  • Fragmented POS. Floor, kitchen, and bar live in different tools. Managers close the night in a spreadsheet.
  • Host + operator venues. The guest is asked to pay twice. Tips, comps, and disputes cannot be split cleanly.
  • Multiple payment brands. Stall operators bring their own processors. The house loses the receipt and any fair cut.
  • Settlement after the fact. A $35 dispute fee, when it exists, is not allocated by who actually sold the food.

Summex’s answer is one system for service and money movement.

3. What Summex is

Summex is a single application. The public site is product, pricing, the Operators Guide, and Sign in — not a dashboard. Floor work is PIN on a primed station. Integrations never offer Stripe, Square, or similar as a POS processor. Hours-export partners are not card processors, and Summex does not process payroll. This paper does not document control-plane CRM or pipeline internals.

4. Operating models

Single-operator

One brand owns the location. That brand is its own Quantum Payments merchant. The ledger still records captures, tips, cash, voids, and (if filed) disputes.

Host + multiple operators

Host Venue owns the floor and the guest-facing brand. Operator A and Operator B own lines, tickets, and period payouts. Each entity is its own Quantum Payments merchant. The guest sees one check and one tender. Capture splits by merchandise owner. Printed receipts group lines under the vendor name — still one document. A bar-scoped operator sees bar cost and sales; a food-scoped operator sees food; the host sees the house pack.

5. Guest experience

  • One check, even when food and drink belong to different operators.
  • One guest-facing brand (the location / host name).
  • One card tender on Quantum Payments. The guest never sees Finix.
  • Printed receipts group items by vendor; the guest still holds one check.
  • Printed menu prices stay clean (e.g. $15.00). Cash discount, if posted, is computed — not a second ugly menu.

The guest does not see operator splits, host cut, ledger rows, or device roles.

6. Quantum Payments — per-entity merchants, one guest check

Every card tender runs through Quantum Payments. Each entity (host and each tenant operator) is its own merchant on the Finix rail. Guest UI never names Finix. A brand cannot take live cards until that brand’s application is approved.

The guest tenders once. Capture splits to each brand’s merchant by merchandise owner. Tax, tip, and service allocate by merchandise share. Receipts group lines by vendor.

This is not a second terminal and not a live processor split-payout. Period money movement is a product-owned ledger. Electronic payout rows address an operator’s account placeholder. Live ACH is not claimed.

Training uses Quantum Payments sandbox — practice cards, not a live Visa. Live cards require lifecycle live, an approved application, and an enrolled Quantum reader. If the processor is down: take cash or keep the check open. Card is not queued and never fakes a live Visa.

Roadmap: connecting a live bank rail. Payout last4 in the product is a stub for operations rehearsal.

7. Gift ledger

Gift is a first-party Summex ledger — swipe, scan, or key. It is not Finix and not Quantum Payments. Load requires cash or card on the same ticket. Redeem never calls an outside gift network. House and operators may issue. Outstanding liability is tracked by issuer entity. Adjust, freeze, and deactivate are manager events.

8. Stations — order, ODS, host

The tablet is a screen, not a person. PIN says who is working. The device role says what this screen is for.

RoleScreen
OrderHandhelds and bar POS — menu, checks, pay, gift
ODSKitchen (and bar display) — tickets, Start / Bump. No pay. Cash and gift blocked on ODS.
HostFloor map, seat, table status, to-go at the stand

Prime, then PIN-only. Pair and open the station once from the signed-in control plane (internet required). After that, cold start is the PIN pad — not /login.

PIN ≠ owner password ≠ clock-in ≠ closeout. Back office uses email and password. Floor staff use a 4-digit PIN, hashed and scoped to the location (and entity). Clock in / out is Labor. Server closeout is Cash. Realtime staffing recommendations (cut / hold / add) never clock anyone out — accept offers notify-to-close-out.

Printers sit on the AP LAN (not printer Wi‑Fi): thermal receipts (Epson TM-T20), impact kitchen (Epson TM-U220), drawer kick on the receipt printer.

9. Cash models

  • One-person drawer
  • Shared drawer (one till; cash still reported by user)
  • Server bank
  • Multi-well bar — one drawer per well
  • Host to-go drawer plus floor banks

Count may be blind. Over/short flags a manager queue — not an automatic accusation. Closeout is not clock-out and not PIN login. House Wi‑Fi still records cash if the internet is down. Card requires connection.

10. Tips, tip-out, and pools

Tip-out recommendations follow sales mix (food vs drink), not a flat percent of all sales. Two servers with the same volume do not owe the same kitchen and bar amounts.

Card tips follow a location (or employer-entity) setting:

  • cash_at_close — closeout cash due includes card tips, paid out from the drawer or safe; payroll export does not add those card tips again. Blind expected includes that paid-out.
  • paycheck — closeout shows card tips as informational; cash due from card tips is $0; card tips go on the hours-export file. Summex does not run payroll.
  • cash_tips_only_at_close — only declared cash tips are settled in person; card tips always export to payroll.

Pools are a location (or employer-entity) setting. A pool mode can combine mix-based tip-out. Payout uses cash-at-close vs paycheck.

  • individual — each person keeps their own tips.
  • individual_plus_tipout — keep own tips after mix-based tip-outs.
  • foh_pool — servers, hosts, bussers, cashiers share one pool.
  • bar_pool — bartenders share all wells together, or each well on its own.
  • team_pool — one pool for every included role.
  • dual_pool — food-line ownership funds the FOH pool; drink-line ownership funds the bar pool.

Contribution, split (hours / points / equal / sales / manual), include/exclude roles, managers excluded by default, settle at shift or pay period. Auto-grat stays with the server, enters the pool, or splits. Service charge is house or a percent to staff — never labeled a tip unless that box is checked. Closeout shows own tips, tip-outs, pool in, pool out, net due now vs paycheck. Reports and the hours CSV list net by person and pool.

Pooling rules vary by state. Summex calculates the configured policy only — not legal advice and not a payroll run. Hours, OT, declared and card tips export to ADP, Intuit, or CSV. Staffing recommendations are cut / hold / add only — never auto clock-out.

11. Cash discount model

Printed / card price is the source of truth. When cash discount is enabled: cashRaw = printed × (1 − percent/100), then round up to the increment ($0.25, $0.50, $0.75, or $1.00). Exact multiples stay.

Printed / cardRaw 5%Cash (round up $0.25)
$15.00$14.25$14.25
$12.00$11.40$11.50
$7.00$6.65$6.75

Quantum Payments still captures the printed amount. The house is responsible for local cash-discount posting rules.

12. Training vs live

A live Summex starts empty. There is no public demo tenant. Training is the real POS with Quantum Payments sandbox. Go live is an explicit owner action. Live Visa is blocked until lifecycle is live, plus an approved application and enrolled reader.

13. Fees and disputes

Software fees are quoted in intake — not card-processing rates. Card fee % on settlement is a house-configured estimate on the period ledger, not a guaranteed Quantum Payments schedule.

Quantum Payments charges $35.00 only when a real dispute is filed on a closed check with a card capture. Mixed merchandise (e.g. $65 food / $35 drink) splits the fee 65/35 → $22.75 / $12.25. No dispute → $0. Won or lost does not reverse the $35. Exception reports are a review queue, not verdicts. The product does not accuse staff of theft.

14. Security, roles, and audit

PIN access level controls POS tools. Account membership controls the back office. Platform Admin is not a restaurant owner. Paid checks freeze. Gift adjust is manager-only. The audit log and system ledger are append-only. This paper does not claim PCI DSS, SOC, or other seals. Summex does not store full PAN on guest profiles.

15. Software subscription vs processing

Software billing is not guest card processing.

  • Base software (counter-service POS + kitchen display) is $0 / month. Tablets, printers, drawers, stands, and gift MSR are BYO. Live cards require Finix/Quantum readers supplied through Summex (~$75 default, settings price). Customer-owned Square/Stripe/bank terminals are not supported. Optional partner extras are typically more expensive than BYO. Software monthly is never mixed with hardware.
  • Paid packages add monthly software for features selected at intake (floor/host, kiosk, QR, labor/HR, recipes/costing, multi-entity, and similar).
  • Setup is optional (waive, flat, or by package) and is never the only line on a proposal.
  • Email (quotes, invites, receipts) is included — never surcharged.
  • SMS is allotted 500 texts / location / month (editable). Extra at a pass-through rate, or block at cap. Location can turn SMS off or set a lower cap. Waitlist confirm, table-ready, and opt-out each count 1. Alerts at 80% and 100%.
  • AI reports ship with the Ops pack (Get-a-price interview always allowed) and are throttled per location per day so jobs cannot loop.
  • Quantum Payments (and cash-discount settings) are a separate note, not mixed into the software monthly unless the house chose a bundled plan.

Get a price shows: “Email included. SMS: 500/mo included, extra at cost. AI reports in Ops pack.” A proposal includes package name, intake feature list, monthly software, any setup, entity and station counts, and an expires-on date. Accept or request changes. Onboarding waits until a monthly package quote exists and is accepted. This paper is not a rate card.

16. Implementation path

  1. The house is onboarded as a location.
  2. Owner or manager primes each tablet once while online (Open POS).
  3. Thereafter the station is PIN-only.
  4. Training week with sandbox cards. Configure floor, menu, devices, cash, tips.
  5. Go live when the house is ready — approved Quantum application and enrolled reader for live cards.

Roadmap (not in this build): live ACH/payout rails, QuickBooks sync, PCI audit letter, guaranteed processor rates.
